[DL] Does the combined method deliver positive savings across all use cases?
GreenPT POC


1. Context

Project: GreenPT benchmarking POC / day 2 (interpreting full-run results, second key finding).

Why this matters right now:
The simplest pitch for GreenPT was "combined dominates everywhere — it stacks both optimization
layers, so it has to win." The full run breaks that pitch. combined is the quality champion (mean
G-Eval 4.29 vs 3.14 for baseline) but its savings number is wildly variable per use case —
strongly positive on nested schemas, deeply negative on single-field intent-classification outputs.
This forces an honest reframe and a concrete routing rule. A client pitched "combined is always
best" and then running it on their chatbot intent classifier would lose 36% of tokens while paying
top quality scores. That's not a great first impression.

Where this fits:
Together with DL-03 (prompt_engineering verbose), provides the data that DL-05 (smart routing v1)
needs. Also shapes the report framing in DL-07 — "combined is the quality champion; smart routing
picks the right method per schema" is the honest version of the pitch.

2. Research question

Does the combined method (green-l + client-side TOON with a deterministic key_map) deliver
positive savings across all use cases — i.e., is "always use combined" a defensible blanket
recommendation?


3. Current LO stage

[x] Realizing  — ran the full 192-row benchmark
[x] Managing   — read the per-use-case pivots, found the variance, traced it to a structural
                  cost in the combined method's construction


4. What makes this a valid falsification

- The variance shows up at the use_case level, not just per-row noise — 12 rows per cell
- Quality is preserved or improved across the variance — quality champion status holds even on
  negative-savings rows
- The cause is mechanical: traceable to a specific cost structure in the method, not a model whim


5. What I decided

Treat this as a confirmed finding. combined is the quality champion but not a universal savings
winner. Use it as the default for nested / multi-field schemas (invoice, resume). Fall back to
postprocess for tiny-output workloads (intent classification, short summarization). Codify this
in smart_route v1 (DL-05).


6. Why this decision

Method: Lab (full 48-row x 4-method run, then per-use-case pivot in outputs/results.csv).

The full results split combined into two regimes:

  use_case               combined savings vs baseline    G-Eval
  ---------------------- ----------------------------    ------
  invoice_parsing            +32.1%                       3.83
  resume_parsing             +13.8%                       4.33
  summarization              -2.3%                        4.50
  intent_classification     -36.0%                        4.58

Mean combined savings is only +2.0% across the dataset — dragged down by intent_classification
and summarization.

[IMAGE: outputs/chart_savings.png — Bar chart showing per-method mean savings. combined's bar
is close to zero despite winning big on invoice_parsing; the per-use-case table is where the
story lives.]

[IMAGE: outputs/chart_quality.png — Bar chart showing per-method mean G-Eval. combined is the
clear quality leader at 4.29, a full point above the other methods at ~3.14-3.19.]

The mechanism is mechanical. The combined method prepends a TOON-instruction block to the user
message because green-l rejects system prompts (A1 finding). That block lives in
@tools/greentpt_tools.py:45-56 as _toon_user_prefix — approximately 80 tokens of instruction
overhead injected into every combined call:

    "OUTPUT INSTRUCTIONS — apply to your JSON answer below:\n"
    f"1. Rename keys using this exact map: {km_json}\n"
    "2. Output minified JSON — no whitespace, no newlines.\n"
    "3. Never include the map in your output. No markdown. No code fences.\n\n"
    "TASK:\n"

For an invoice with 5 nested line-item arrays, that ~80-token overhead is amortized across ~150
tokens of compressed output — net positive savings.

For an intent_classification output like {"intent": "password_reset"}, the same instruction
overhead lands on top of a ~30-token baseline output. The overhead is bigger than the entire
baseline output. combined loses on savings even while gaining on quality (the explicit minify
instruction is what pushes the score to 4.58).

Said simply: combined trades a fixed ~80-token instruction overhead for percentage-based savings
on the output. The trade only pays when the output is big enough that the percentage savings
exceed the fixed overhead. Below that threshold, instructions cost more than they save.

What I'd recommend:
Three things.

  1. Codify the threshold in smart_route v1: if the schema has very few keys or very short keys,
     skip combined and use postprocess. Rough threshold from the data: fewer than ~5 keys x
     6-char avg key length (@sdk.py).
  2. Product follow-up: move the TOON-instruction block into the GreenPT system prompt
     server-side. The overhead disappears from the on-the-wire user message and combined would
     be net-positive everywhere. This is a GreenPT-side change, not a client one.
  3. Report framing: for clients with invoice / resume / long-form workloads, lead with combined.
     For chatbot / intent-classifier customers, lead with postprocess and disclose the overhead
     floor up front.

GAP found:
We don't yet know whether the overhead scales the same way on the reasoning tier (green-r). The
benchmark only covered green-l. A short follow-up run on green-r — same 48 rows, same methodology,
~10 minutes — would tell us whether the routing thresholds need to differ by model tier.

- Quality champion status preserved:    ✅ combined highest-scoring on 4/4 use

- Cause is mechanical:                  |  _toon_user_prefix overhead traceable in
                                            @tools/greentpt_tools.py:45-56 — but

                                            is probably also stacked on top. Two
                                            effects not ablated separately. Dominant
                                            mechanism is the ~80-token prefix; exact
                                            split unverified.

❌ Original assumption ("combined dominates everywhere") — falsified; holds only for nested /
   multi-field schemas.

Assumptions I'm making:
The TOON-instruction block is the dominant overhead source. It's possible green-l's own response
style also adds tokens on short outputs (DL-03 confirms green-l is more verbose than green-l-raw).
The two effects are probably stacked on intent_classification — disentangling them would need a
separate ablation run.

What surprised me:
The quality story going the opposite direction from the savings story. combined scored 4.58 on
intent_classification — the highest quality score of any method x use_case combination in the
entire run — while losing 36% on savings for that same combination. That tension is exactly the
kind of honest trade-off a report should name out loud, not paper over.

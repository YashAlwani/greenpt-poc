Research question
-----------------
What artifacts — and in what format — are needed to turn 192 rows of benchmark results into
a client-ready story, given that the benchmark may re-run and artifacts must never go stale?


Recommendation
--------------
  Artifact 1: Auto-generated PDF report (report.py)
     Format:   .pdf generated via reportlab
     Audience: technical stakeholder / CTO / procurement team
     When:     auto-regenerated every time results.csv changes

  Artifact 2: Editable client demo deck (make_deck.py)
     Format:   .pptx generated via python-pptx
     Audience: sales meeting, exec sponsor, non-technical champion
     When:     generated once, customized per meeting by sales

Do not use: Google Slides link, Notion page, or a hardcoded PDF. These require manual updates
whenever results change.


Why these formats
-----------------
Method: Library (compared format options for each artifact type against the auto-regen and
editability requirements).

Why reportlab PDF for the technical report:

  Markdown / README: version-controlled, easy to write, but does not look like a deliverable
  in a client context. Not printable or e-mailable as a standalone artifact. Rejected.

  Jupyter Notebook: good for inline charts, but requires the recipient to run Python to see the
  charts. nbconvert to PDF is fragile and environment-dependent. Rejected.

  Google Docs / Notion: easy to share via link, but cannot be auto-regenerated from Python
  without API integration. Introduces a manual update step exactly where errors live. Rejected.

  reportlab PDF (selected): purely programmatic — report.py reads results.csv and writes
  report.pdf. Re-running report.py always reflects the latest data. No login, no browser, no
  sharing link. Works offline. The number in the PDF is always the number in the CSV.

Why python-pptx for the deck:

  PDF slides (reportlab): non-editable. Sales cannot adjust per client context. Rejected.

  Google Slides: shareable, collaborative, but requires internet and a Google account. Cannot
  be auto-generated from Python without the Slides API. Rejected.

  Canva / Figma export: professional templates, not editable by sales without an account, and
  not script-generatable from benchmark data. Rejected.

  python-pptx (selected): generates an editable PowerPoint file from Python. Sales can open it
  in PowerPoint, Keynote, or Google Slides and customize per meeting. Data-driven: make_deck.py
  reads results.csv and populates headline metrics directly — no manual number entry.


Minimum viable deck structure (recommended 6 slides, expanded to 11 in DL-07):

  Slide 1: Title + client name (editable field)
  Slide 2: What we measured — 4 methods x 4 use cases x 2 sizes, T=0, G-Eval quality scoring
  Slide 3: Headline results table — method / avg_savings / avg_quality (from results.csv)
  Slide 4: Use-case breakdown — Combined saves 36% on invoices, costs 32% on intent tasks.
           Smart routing picks the right method automatically.
  Slide 5: Developer experience — GreenPTClient().call(prompt, schema) -> result.
           Screenshot of playground UI.
  Slide 6: Next steps — editable placeholder; sales fills in per meeting

The deck should NOT include raw CSV data, G-Eval methodology explanation, or code snippets.
Those belong in the PDF report for technical reviewers.


What to watch for during implementation
---------------------------------------
- python-pptx adds a font dependency. Use system-safe fonts (Calibri, Arial) to avoid rendering
  surprises at the client site.
- The headline savings numbers in slide 3 must come from results.csv at generation time, not be
  hardcoded. A hardcoded number that doesn't match the CSV is what a technical client catches
  in the first five minutes.
- Keep all generated outputs in outputs/: results.csv, report.pdf, greenpt_demo.pptx.


Note: the final deck (make_deck.py) was expanded to 11 slides beyond the 6 recommended here.
The core 6-slide structure maps to final slides 1 (title), 5 (how we measured), 6 (results
headline), 7 (use-case breakdown), 9 (developer experience), and 10 (what's next). Additional
slides added: problem (2), what GreenPT does (3), drop-in integration (4), live example (8),
close card (11).
